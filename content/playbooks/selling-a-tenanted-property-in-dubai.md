**On the illustrative one bedroom this site uses throughout, a tenanted sale clears at a discount between AED 22,876 and AED 32,316, which is 1.53% to 2.15% of a AED 1,500,000 price.** Below the bottom of that range the buyer is better off buying the empty unit next door. Above the top of it the seller is better off serving the twelve month notice and selling vacant in a year. The range between them is **0.63 of a percentage point, or AED 9,440**, and it is almost the same width whether the sitting tenant pays the index rent or forty percent below it.

Two things decide whether that range exists at all, and neither of them is the rent. They are what the seller would otherwise do with the money, and how long the lease has left to run. Both are computed below.

Every competing page on this question answers a different one. The brokerage guides confirm that you may sell and list the grounds; the newspaper legal columns answer one reader's letter. None of them prices the choice, which is the only part a seller actually has to make.

## Two grounds, and they are not priced the same

Article 25(2) of Law No. (26) of 2007, as replaced by Law No. (33) of 2008, is a closed list of four reasons a landlord may end a tenancy on expiry. Two of them matter to an owner who wants his unit back. The fourth is the sale ground, and its whole text is that **the leased Real Property owner wishes to sell it**. The third is the own use ground, where the owner **wishes to retake possession of the Real Property for his own use or for use by any of his first-degree relatives**, and it carries a condition the sale ground does not: **provided that the owner proves that he does not own any alternative Real Property that is suitable for this purpose**.

That condition quietly removes the own use ground from most of the people reading this. An investor with a second Dubai apartment cannot prove he owns no suitable alternative. For him the sale ground is not the easier route, it is the only route.

Both grounds run on the same notice. The landlord must notify the tenant of the eviction reasons **at least twelve (12) months before the date of eviction**, and **the notice must be served through a Notary Public or by registered mail**. Service is not a formality. It is the thing a landlord with a good case loses on, and the [rent increase caps](/playbooks/rent-increase-caps/) page carries the other notice period, the ninety days for changing a term on renewal, which is a different provision answering a different question.

## What the seller can afford to give up

Take the apartment from the [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/) page: AED 1,500,000 with vacant possession, 900 square feet, an index rent of AED 105,000. A sitting tenant pays AED 78,750, which is 25% below the index. Fully occupied, that tenancy produces **AED 53,174** of net operating income, using the same service charge, management, maintenance and insurance lines as the flagship page.

The owner has two routes to cash.

**Sell now, tenanted.** He accepts a discount and receives the money today, less the agency commission and the VAT on it, which is 2.1% of the price.

**Serve notice, sell vacant.** He collects twelve months of the sitting rent, then sells the empty unit at the full AED 1,500,000, a year later.

Discounted at 6% a year, the same rate the [off-plan against ready](/playbooks/off-plan-vs-ready/) page uses so one assumption carries across both, the second route is worth AED 51,485 of rent plus AED 1,385,377 of discounted net proceeds. Set that equal to a discounted sale today and the seller's ceiling is exact: **AED 32,316, or 2.15% of the price.** Give away more than that and he should have waited.

## What the buyer needs, and why it is not the rent ladder

The buyer's side is usually argued the other way, as though taking a sitting tenant means inheriting a below-index rent forever. It does not, and the reason is in two provisions.

Article 28 of the 2007 law, which the amendment did not touch, says that **Transferring the ownership of Real Property to a new owner does not affect the Tenant's right to continue to occupy the Real Property by virtue of the Lease Contract entered into with the previous owner, provided that such Lease Contract has a fixed term**. So the tenancy survives the sale. But the definition of Landlord in Article 2 says that **This includes any person to whom the ownership of the Real Property is transferred during the term of lease**, which makes the buyer a landlord on the day he takes title, with Article 25(2) available to him.

That bounds his loss. He is not buying the ladder of capped renewals that the [rent increase caps](/playbooks/rent-increase-caps/) page follows to its terminus. He is buying twelve months of a rent below the index, after which he can have the unit empty on his own notice. On this unit the income the tenancy gives up over those twelve months is AED 23,626, worth **AED 22,876** today, which is **1.53%** of the price. That is his floor.

## The two numbers, side by side

| Sitting rent against the index | Sitting rent, AED | Seller's ceiling, AED | Buyer's floor, AED | The range, AED |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| At the index | 105,000 | 8,949 | 0 | 8,949 |
| 10% below | 94,500 | 18,296 | 9,150 | 9,146 |
| 20% below | 84,000 | 27,642 | 18,300 | 9,342 |
| 25% below | 78,750 | 32,316 | 22,876 | 9,440 |
| 30% below | 73,500 | 36,988 | 27,450 | 9,538 |
| 40% below | 63,000 | 46,334 | 36,600 | 9,734 |

**The range barely moves.** From a tenant paying the index to one paying forty percent under it, the seller's ceiling quadruples and the buyer's floor goes from nothing to AED 36,600, but the gap between them widens only from AED 8,949 to AED 9,734.

The mechanism is not a coincidence. The rent the seller gives up by selling now and the rent the buyer gives up by buying tenanted are the same rent, counted once on each side of the table. Widening the gap moves both columns together and leaves the difference almost untouched. So the sentence an agent reaches for, that a cheap sitting tenant is what makes a tenanted sale hard, is the wrong way round: a cheap tenant moves the price, not the deal.

## What decides it is the seller's cost of money

What is left in the arithmetic once the tenancy drops out is the difference between a year of interest on the sale proceeds and a year of net rent. That is the whole of the bargaining range, and it is set by the discount rate.

| Seller's money would otherwise earn | Seller's ceiling | Buyer's floor | The range, points | Does a tenanted sale clear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3% a year | -0.65% | 1.55% | -2.20 | No |
| 4% a year | 0.30% | 1.54% | -1.24 | No |
| 5% a year | 1.24% | 1.53% | -0.30 | No |
| 6% a year | 2.15% | 1.53% | 0.63 | Yes |
| 7% a year | 3.05% | 1.52% | 1.54 | Yes |
| 8% a year | 3.94% | 1.51% | 2.43 | Yes |
| 9% a year | 4.81% | 1.50% | 3.31 | Yes |

At 3% the seller's ceiling is **negative**. There is no discount at which selling tenanted beats waiting, because a year of the sitting rent is worth more than a year of interest on the proceeds, so he should serve the notice and sell vacant even if a tenanted buyer offers the full asking price.

The rate at which the range opens is **5.32%**, and across every rent gap in the table above it sits between 5.30% and 5.35%. That number is not arbitrary either. The unit's own net operating income over what a sale actually nets is **5.23%**, and the threshold is essentially that yield.

**So the question to ask a seller is not how far below market his tenant is. It is what the money will do next.** If it is going to sit on deposit below the property's own net yield, the property is the better place for it for one more year and the notice is the right move. If it has somewhere better to go, a tenanted sale clears and the range is wide. No competing page on this query asks the question at all, and it is the only input that changes the answer rather than the price.

## The input a listing never mentions

Article 25(2) permits eviction on expiry of the lease contract, so the earliest a buyer can have the unit empty is the later of the twelve month notice and the end of the contract he inherits. A tenancy with two years left costs him two years of the shortfall, not one.

| Unexpired lease, months | Months the buyer waits | Buyer's floor, AED | Seller's ceiling, AED | The range, AED |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 12 | 22,876 | 32,316 | 9,440 |
| 12 | 12 | 22,876 | 32,316 | 9,440 |
| 18 | 18 | 33,810 | 32,316 | -1,494 |
| 24 | 24 | 44,423 | 32,316 | -12,107 |
| 36 | 36 | 64,718 | 32,316 | -32,402 |

**The deal stops clearing at about eighteen months of unexpired term**, and on this unit at this rate the exact crossing is between seventeen and eighteen months. At a wider rent gap it comes sooner, at sixteen months on a tenant forty percent below the index, and at a narrow gap it comes beyond two years.

This is the practical finding on the page. A freshly signed two year tenancy is worth more damage to a sale price than a tenant paying far under the index, and it is the fact a listing never carries. Ask for the tenancy contract and its expiry date before making an offer, which is the same advice the [due diligence](/playbooks/due-diligence-before-an-offer/) page gives about the title and for the same reason.

## What a notice already served is worth

If the twelve month notice has been running before the unit is listed, the buyer waits only for what is left of it, and his floor falls with every month already served.

| Months of notice already run | Months the buyer still waits | Buyer's floor, AED | What the served notice is worth, AED |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 12 | 22,876 | 0 |
| 3 | 9 | 17,285 | 5,591 |
| 6 | 6 | 11,609 | 11,267 |
| 9 | 3 | 5,848 | 17,028 |
| 12 | 0 | 0 | 22,876 |

**A notice served six months before the unit is listed is worth about AED 11,267 to the price, and one fully run is worth AED 22,876.** On this unit that is a piece of paper carrying roughly 0.75% to 1.53% of the sale price, which is more than the trustee, title deed and map fees on the way in cost altogether.

One honest qualification, and it is the reason this table is a range rather than a figure. The law makes the transferee a landlord and preserves the tenant's occupation under a fixed term contract. It does not say in terms whether a notice served by the previous owner runs for the new one. That is a question of practice rather than of text, so the value above is contingent, and the thing to do with it is get the position in writing before paying for the paper rather than assume it either way.

What is not contingent is the timing. A seller who intends vacant possession and has not served notice has already lost a year, and that is the one mistake on this page which no price can fix later.

## The own use ground is the expensive one

An owner who evicts in order to live in the unit rather than sell it buys a restriction. Article 26 says that where the tribunal rules in his favour on the own use ground, the landlord **may not rent the Real Property to a third party before the lapse of at least two (2) years in case of residential Real Property, or three (3) years in case of non-residential Real Property**, and otherwise **the Tenant may request the Tribunal to award him fair compensation**.

Priced on this unit, at the index rent, two years of letting foregone is AED 153,600 of net operating income, worth **AED 144,402** today. Three years, on a commercial unit, is AED 230,400 nominal and AED 210,375 in today's money.

Nothing comparable attaches to the sale ground. So the two grounds are not interchangeable routes to the same outcome: choosing to say you want to live in it, rather than that you want to sell it, costs you the option to change your mind for two years, and on this flat that option is worth 9.63% of the price.

## If the tenant does not leave

The fee for making him leave is small and published. The Rental Disputes Centre charges **3.5% of Annual Rent or Lease Value if it is more or less than a year**, with a **Fee not less than (AED 500) and not more than (AED 20,000)**, plus AED 100 of process service and AED 20 of knowledge and innovation fees. On the sitting rent of AED 78,750 that is a registration fee of AED 2,756 and **AED 2,876 all in, which is 0.19% of the sale price.**

And the rent does not stop while it runs. Article 31 of the 2007 law says that **Filing a claim to evict the Tenant does not exempt the Tenant from paying the Rent for the whole period during which the claim is considered, and until an award is rendered and executed**.

Put those two together and the shape of the risk is clear. Enforcing an eviction is cheap in fees and the income continues, so what an overstay costs is neither the fee nor the rent. It is time, and time is the one thing the table above prices at roughly AED 1,900 a month of buyer's floor on this unit. A seller who needs a date should assume the date is the eviction date plus a dispute, and price the sale, not the fee.

## The honest summary

Sell tenanted when the money has somewhere better to go than the property, when the lease has a year or less to run, and at a discount inside the AED 22,876 to AED 32,316 band that this unit's arithmetic supports. Serve notice and sell vacant when the money would otherwise earn less than the unit's own net yield of about 5.23%, or when the tenancy has eighteen months or more left, because in both cases there is no price a tenanted buyer can rationally pay that beats waiting.

And whichever you choose, choose it a year before you list. The fee side of the sale is on [the cost of selling property in Dubai](/playbooks/selling-well/) and the round trip against a holding period is on [transaction costs and the minimum hold period](/playbooks/transaction-cost-drag/), but neither of them can recover a notice that was never served.
