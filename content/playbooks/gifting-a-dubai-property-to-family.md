Registering a gift of a Dubai apartment worth AED 1,500,000 to a spouse or a child costs **AED 4,620** in Land Department and trustee fees, on the fees published in Executive Council Resolution No. (30) of 2013 and on the Land Department's own gift registration service page. Registering the same flat as a sale between the same two people costs **AED 64,720**. The gift route is **7.1%** of the sale route, and every figure on this page is added up from those two schedules rather than quoted from anybody.

Every page on this query gives you the comparison as two percentages, 0.125% against 4%, and leaves it there. The 4% is a rate. The 0.125% is not, for most of the Dubai apartment market, and the reason is one clause in the Schedule that no competing page quotes.

## The floor under the famous rate

Row 5 of the Schedule to [Executive Council Resolution No. (30) of 2013](/dubai-property-law/) prices registering a gift at **0.125% of the value of the Real Property given as a gift, provided that the Fee is not less than AED 2,000.00**. The second half is the whole finding. A percentage with a dirham floor under it stops being a percentage below the point where the two meet, and that point is arithmetic rather than opinion:

    AED 2,000 / 0.00125 = AED 1,600,000

Below a valuation of AED 1,600,000 the Land Department's fee is a flat AED 2,000, and its share of the property rises as the property gets cheaper.

| Valuation | Gift fee | Share of the valuation | Which half of row 5 applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500,000 | 2,000 | 0.400% | the floor |
| 800,000 | 2,000 | 0.250% | the floor |
| 1,000,000 | 2,000 | 0.200% | the floor |
| 1,500,000 | 2,000 | 0.133% | the floor |
| 1,600,000 | 2,000 | 0.125% | they meet |
| 2,000,000 | 2,500 | 0.125% | the rate |
| 3,000,000 | 3,750 | 0.125% | the rate |
| 5,000,000 | 6,250 | 0.125% | the rate |

So 0.125% is the lowest the gift fee ever gets, not the price of the transaction. On the one bedroom the [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/) page prices at AED 1,500,000 it is 0.133%, and the median Dubai apartment sits well inside the band where the floor binds. This is the same shape the [refinancing](/playbooks/refinancing-a-dubai-mortgage/) page finds in the early settlement cap, running the other way: a flat ceiling makes a percentage fall as the number grows, a flat floor makes it fall as the number grows too, and in both cases the published rate describes only one end of the market.

## The whole stack, which is one number

The fee is not the bill. The Land Department's own gift registration service page publishes the rest, and its service terms say a valuation comes first: **A property evaluation request must be submitted at one of the Real Estate Trustee Centers before applying for registration**.

| Line | Amount | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| Registering a gift, 0.125% of the valuation | 2,000 | ECR 30 of 2013, Schedule row 5, the floor binds |
| Title deed certificate | 250 | ECR 30 of 2013, Schedule; DLD gift service page |
| Map, apartments and villas | 250 | DLD gift service page |
| Knowledge fee | 10 | DLD gift service page |
| Innovation fee | 10 | DLD gift service page |
| Land Department service partner, with VAT | 2,100 | DLD gift service page, below its AED 2,000,000 threshold |
| **Total** | **4,620** | **0.308% of the illustrative valuation** |

Every line in that table is a flat amount at this valuation. The consequence is worth stating plainly, because no page on this query states it: **below a valuation of AED 1,600,000 the entire cost of a Dubai gift transfer is AED 4,620, whatever the property is worth.** A studio and a AED 1,500,000 apartment pay the same AED 4,620. As a share of value that is 0.924% on the studio and 0.308% on the apartment, and the owner of the studio is paying three times the rate for the identical six lines.

One line is deliberately missing. The service terms require the trustee centre valuation, the Land Department publishes no price for it, and this site holds no dataset for it, so it is named rather than estimated. The AED 4,620 is a floor on the cost of a gift transfer, in the same way the [cost of buying](/playbooks/cost-of-buying-in-dubai/) page's totals are floors.

## The threshold that is not the sale threshold

The service partner fee is the trustee office's own charge, which is why it carries VAT where the registration fee does not. Its amount is the same on both routes and its threshold is not.

| | Gift registration | Sale registration |
|---|---|---|
| Fee at or above the threshold | 4,000 + VAT | 4,000 + VAT |
| Fee below it | 2,000 + VAT | 2,000 + VAT |
| Where the threshold sits | 2,000,000 | 500,000 |

Between AED 500,000 and AED 2,000,000 of value, a gift pays AED 2,100 for the office visit and a sale pays AED 4,200. That covers most of the Dubai apartment market, and no page on this query mentions either threshold.

It also puts the only discontinuity in the stack at AED 2,000,000 rather than where a reader would look for it. At a valuation of AED 1,999,999 the gift costs **AED 5,120**. At AED 2,000,000 it costs **AED 7,220**. One dirham of valuation adds **AED 2,100**, and because the step is larger than the 0.125% earns over that range, the cost of a gift as a share of value is **higher** at AED 2,000,000, at **0.361%**, than it is at AED 1,600,000, at **0.289%**. The curve goes up before it comes down again.

## Against registering it as a sale

Both totals below are fees only. No agency commission appears on either side: a transfer inside a family needs no agent, and putting one on the sale route would flatter the gift route with a cost the sale route does not have to carry either. Article 3(1) makes the sale fee **shared equally by the seller and purchaser** unless the contract says otherwise; Article 3(5) makes the gift fee **paid by the Person to whom the rights are transferred**, so on a gift it is the recipient's bill by default and not the donor's. The sale column below assumes the buyer carries the whole 4%, which is the Dubai convention and not the Article's default.

| Valuation | Gift | Sale | Gap | Gift as a share of the sale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500,000 | 4,620 | 24,720 | 20,100 | 18.7% |
| 800,000 | 4,620 | 36,720 | 32,100 | 12.6% |
| 1,000,000 | 4,620 | 44,720 | 40,100 | 10.3% |
| 1,500,000 | 4,620 | 64,720 | 60,100 | 7.1% |
| 1,600,000 | 4,620 | 68,720 | 64,100 | 6.7% |
| 2,000,000 | 7,220 | 84,720 | 77,500 | 8.5% |
| 3,000,000 | 8,470 | 124,720 | 116,250 | 6.8% |
| 5,000,000 | 10,970 | 204,720 | 193,750 | 5.4% |

The gap is real and it is large. What the right-hand column shows is that the relief is worth proportionally more the more expensive the property: on a AED 5,000,000 villa the gift costs 5.4% of what the sale costs, on a AED 500,000 studio 18.7%, because the flat AED 2,000 floor and the flat AED 520 of certificates are a bigger share of a small bill.

None of which makes a gift a cheaper sale. The service is for a transfer **without compensation, provided that the property is not restricted or granted land**, and the people it is open to are a closed published list: **first-degree relatives; mother, father, spouse, or children or to companies**. Siblings, grandparents and cousins are not named on it. A payment passing the other way makes the transaction a sale whatever the deed is called, and the 4% in the table is what a sale costs.

## The same rate buys a will

The company limb of that list is the one worth reading twice, because it is the mechanism behind the structuring pitch the [company or own name](/playbooks/corporate-vs-personal-ownership/) page prices: moving a property you own into an entity is a registration, and the rate on that registration is the gift rate rather than the sale rate. That page is where the holding costs of the structure are added up, and they are the larger number.

The other comparison the Schedule invites is with doing nothing yet. Row 33 prices registering a will at **0.125% of the value of the Real Property, which is the subject matter of the will**: the same rate as the gift, and on that row without the AED 2,000 floor. So the Land Department charges the same 0.125% to move the property now as to record who gets it later, which means the choice between the two is a question about control, timing and certainty rather than about fees. It is not a question this page settles. A will registered with the Land Department is a different instrument from a will registered with the DIFC Courts, which is the register the [UAE wills and succession](/playbooks/uae-wills-and-succession/) page prices and the one that applies to a non-Muslim owner who wants his own law to govern his estate. The two rows of the Schedule tell you the fees are identical. They do not tell you which instrument you need, and anyone who tells you the 0.125% settles that is selling something.

## What this does not settle

Three things sit outside the arithmetic and each of them can be larger than it.

A mortgage. Ownership cannot move while a registered mortgage sits on the title, the discharge is its own Land Department transaction at its own published fee, and the [refinancing](/playbooks/refinancing-a-dubai-mortgage/) page prices that side to the dirham. Whether the lender agrees to any of it is a credit decision that no schedule sets.

Tax somewhere else. The UAE charges no gift tax in either direction, which is why nothing on this page optimises for one. The donor's and the recipient's countries of residence may take a different view of a transfer of an overseas asset, and that question belongs to the [residency and tax](/playbooks/residency-and-tax/) page rather than to a Dubai fee schedule.

And the asset itself. A gift is not a fee-planning manoeuvre, it is a change of owner. The donor's rent, the donor's ability to borrow against the flat and the donor's ability to sell it all end on registration, and the [round trip](/playbooks/transaction-cost-drag/) that a later sale by the recipient will cost is unchanged by how the property arrived. The AED 60,100 saved on the illustrative apartment is the cheapest part of the decision and the only part anybody writes about.
