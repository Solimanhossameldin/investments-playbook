The comparison is nearly always made on nightly rate multiplied by three hundred and sixty five, against the annual rent. That calculation has never been true for any property anywhere. What follows is the same comparison run net, with every statutory cost taken from the instrument that sets it rather than from a brochure.

## The costs Dubai law actually imposes

These are not estimates. Each is set by a published Dubai instrument, and the ones that matter to the arithmetic are the last two.

| Cost | Amount | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| Holiday home permit, issue or renew | AED 300 per bedroom, capped at AED 1,200 a year | Resolution 49 of 2014 |
| Inspection, and re-inspection | AED 300 per unit | Resolution 49 of 2014 |
| Classification certificate | AED 50 per unit | Resolution 49 of 2014 |
| Tourism dirham, standard holiday home | AED 10 per occupied room per night | Resolution 2 of 2014 |
| Tourism dirham, deluxe holiday home | AED 15 per occupied room per night | Resolution 2 of 2014 |

Three things follow that most pages on this question get wrong.

**The tourism dirham is charged per room, not per unit.** A two bedroom standard holiday home accrues twice what a one bedroom does on the same night. It is collected from the guest and remitted to the Department, and it has to be paid **before the sixteenth day of the month following collection**.

**The permit is per bedroom too**, at AED 300 per bedroom and capped at AED 1,200 a year, which is the cap a four bedroom villa reaches and a studio never does.

**You cannot bill the guest for utilities.** There is an AED 2,000 fine for charging a guest for electricity or water. Cooling a Dubai apartment through August is therefore a cost of the strategy, in the month it is largest, and it is the single line most often left out of a projection.

The rest of the schedule is the shape of the regime rather than the shape of the arithmetic: an AED 5,000 fine for operating without a licence, an AED 500 fine for letting rooms or beds rather than the whole unit, and a doubling of any fine repeated inside a year. Permits run for one year and renew. Classification is deluxe or standard, and it is the classification that sets the tourism dirham.

## The same unit, run both ways

One bedroom. The annual rent, the service charge and the nightly rate below are illustrative and yours will differ. Every deduction is either a rate you can go and quote, or a statutory figure from the table above. The arithmetic is computed by the build, not typed, so the totals cannot drift from the lines.

**As an annual tenancy**

| Line | AED |
|---|---|
| Annual rent | 105,000 |
| Service charge | (14,000) |
| Letting and management, 5% | (5,250) |
| Maintenance reserve | (3,000) |
| Vacancy allowance, 4% | (4,200) |
| **Net** | **78,550** |

**As a standard holiday home**, at AED 750 a night and 70% occupancy, which is 255 nights sold, at an average stay of three nights, so 85 turnovers.

| Line | AED |
|---|---|
| Gross, 750 x 255 nights | 191,250 |
| Operator and platform, 20% of gross | (38,250) |
| Cleaning, 85 stays at 150 | (12,750) |
| Utilities, cooling, internet, consumables | (18,000) |
| Tourism dirham, 10 x 255 nights | (2,550) |
| Holiday home permit, one bedroom | (300) |
| Service charge | (14,000) |
| Maintenance and replacement | (8,000) |
| Furnishing, 75,000 over five years | (15,000) |
| Total deductions | (108,850) |
| **Net** | **82,400** |

The short let grossed **AED 86,250** more than the annual tenancy. It netted **AED 3,850** more. A gap of eighty six thousand became a gap of under four, and the strategy that produced it also produced eighty five check-ins, a licence, a monthly filing and a furnished apartment that is harder to sell.

That is not an argument against short letting. It is an argument against comparing the gross figures, which is the comparison almost every projection puts in front of an owner.

## Solve it backwards, which is the useful direction

Sort the short let costs by how they behave and the whole thing collapses into three numbers. On the unit above: **20% of gross** goes to the operator, **AED 60 a night** goes on tourism dirham and cleaning, and **AED 55,300 a year** is fixed whether the unit sells three hundred nights or thirty.

From there two questions answer themselves.

**At AED 750 a night, how full does it have to be to merely draw level with the annual tenancy?** 248 nights, which is 67.9% occupancy. Every night below that, the annual tenancy was the better decision.

**At 70% occupancy, what nightly rate has to hold all year?** AED 732 a night, held all year. Not the peak rate in March. The average across the soft season too.

Neither number is comfortable, and neither is visible anywhere in a gross comparison. This is the same calculation as [break-even occupancy](/playbooks/break-even-occupancy/), run on a cost stack that is roughly three times larger.

## What occupancy is actually worth

Holding the nightly rate at AED 750 and moving only how full the unit is:

| Occupancy | Nights sold | Net, AED |
|---|---|---|
| 55% | 200 | 52,650 |
| 60% | 219 | 62,960 |
| 65% | 237 | 72,680 |
| 70% | 255 | 82,400 |
| 75% | 273 | 92,120 |
| 80% | 292 | 102,430 |

The annual tenancy nets 78,550 and does it without an occupancy assumption at all. Ten points of occupancy is worth about AED 20,000 here, which is why the operator's forecast is the number to interrogate hardest, and why an operator paid on gross has no reason to interrogate it at all.

## The question you will ask next

**Does the rent cap apply to a short let?** No. The RERA rental index governs renewal increases on an annual tenancy, which is a constraint on the long let and part of what makes its income predictable. The short let has no cap and no floor. See [rent increase caps](/playbooks/rent-increase-caps/).

**Does the service charge change?** No. It is levied on the unit, not the use, and it is the same line on both sides of the comparison. What changes is your ability to absorb it in a soft quarter. See [service charges and reserves](/playbooks/service-charge-and-reserves/).

**What about VAT?** Short term accommodation is a taxable supply in the UAE, and whether you cross the registration threshold is a question for the Federal Tax Authority rather than for a listing agent. It is not modelled above, and if it applies to you it moves the answer against the short let.

**Can the building stop me?** Yes, and this is the cheapest check on the list. Ask the owners association before you furnish, not after. It belongs in the same pass as everything in [due diligence before an offer](/playbooks/due-diligence-before-an-offer/).

## The part that is not financial

An annual tenancy is one signature and four cheques. A short let is a business: pricing, calendar, reviews, guest messages at midnight, a cleaner who did not show up, a permit to renew and a return to file every month. Paying an operator to absorb that is exactly what the twenty percent buys, which is why the comparison after paying an operator is the honest one for most owners.

Run it on your own numbers with the [net rental yield calculator](/calculators/net-rental-yield/), which takes the long let side and gives you the figure the short let has to beat.
