The question usually arrives as a suggestion from someone who earns a fee for establishing the structure. The answer is arithmetic, and the arithmetic contradicts both sides of the argument people actually have about it.

## The short answer

An individual letting a Dubai flat on ordinary tenancy contracts pays no UAE corporate tax on the rent at any income, and a company holding the same flat pays 9% only on taxable income above AED 375,000, which the illustrative one bedroom's AED 69,127 does not reach.

So the brokerage line, no tax on rental income, is right for the individual by a route it never names. And the structuring firm's line, a company costs you nine percent, is right about the rate and wrong about the bill.

**Cabinet Decision No. 116 of 2022 sets the zero per cent band at AED 375,000 of taxable income, so a company holding the illustrative AED 1,500,000 Dubai one bedroom priced on this site, which earns AED 69,127 a year in net operating income, pays no UAE corporate tax on it at all.**

What a company costs a small landlord is therefore not tax. It is formation, licensing, audit and filing, none of which this page prices, because nobody publishes a schedule for them.

## The individual is outside the charge, at any size

Cabinet Decision No. 49 of 2023 sets a turnover test for individuals. A business or business activity conducted by a natural person "shall be subject to Corporate Tax only where the total Turnover derived from such Businesses or Business Activities exceeds AED 1,000,000" in a Gregorian calendar year.

Then Article 2 takes letting out of the test entirely. Wage, personal investment income and real estate investment income "shall not be considered as Businesses or Business Activities conducted by a resident or non-resident natural person subject to Corporate Tax, regardless of the amount of Turnover derived from such activities". The last six words are the ones that matter. An individual with forty flats is as far outside the charge as an individual with one.

The definition carries the only test that can put him back inside it. Real estate investment means activity related to "the sale, leasing, sub-leasing, and renting of land or real estate property in the State that is not conducted, or does not require to be conducted through a Licence from a Licensing Authority". Ordinary tenancy contracts require no licence. Registering a tenancy is a registration, not a licence.

And a natural person outside the charge on this route "shall not be required to register for Corporate Tax". No registration, no return, no filing.

## The company is inside the charge, and usually owes nothing

The corporate tax rate has two limbs. Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 charges "0% (zero percent) on the portion of the Taxable Income not exceeding the amount specified in a decision issued by the Cabinet" and "9% (nine percent) on Taxable Income that exceeds the amount specified in a decision issued by the Cabinet".

Cabinet Decision No. 116 of 2022 supplies the amount, twice over. For the lower limb, "the portion of Taxable Income of the Taxable Person not exceeding (375,000) three hundred seventy-five thousand dirhams shall be subject to Corporate Tax at the rate of (0%) zero percent". For the upper, "the Taxable Income of a Taxable Person that exceeds the amount of (375,000) three hundred seventy-five thousand dirhams shall be subject to Corporate Tax at (9%) nine percent".

So the whole charge is one line: nine percent of whatever is above AED 375,000, and nothing at all below it.

Run the illustrative one bedroom from the [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/) page through it. AED 1,500,000, let at AED 105,000, AED 69,127 of net operating income after vacancy, service charge, management, maintenance and insurance. The company is given the same net operating income as the individual, which flatters the company.

Six of the illustrative one bedroom, AED 9,000,000 of property, is where the first dirham of corporate tax falls due, because five of them earn AED 345,635 and stay inside the AED 375,000 band. The exact crossing is 5.42 units, AED 8,137,197 of property at this unit's economics.

At six units the tax is AED 3,579 a year on AED 9,000,000 of property. That is 0.04 percentage points of net yield, against a service charge spread of 0.98 points between the cheapest and dearest of the 31 buildings the [service charge](/playbooks/service-charge-and-reserves/) page priced. The tax a structure is sold to avoid is one twenty fifth the size of a line nobody shops for.

## The ceiling

As the portfolio grows the band stops mattering and the charge converges on nine percent of net operating income. That is the most UAE corporate tax can ever cost, however large the company gets.

**On this unit's economics the ceiling takes net yield from 4.33% to 3.94%, a cost of 0.39 percentage points a year, and no company owning fewer than six such flats pays anything at all.**

Hold that against the lines this site prices elsewhere. The whole acquisition stack costs 0.28 points of net yield once. A UK higher rate taxpayer's home country tax on the same rent costs 1.73 points a year, on the [tax on Dubai rental income](/playbooks/residency-and-tax/) page. Corporate tax at its absolute ceiling sits between them, and for most readers of the pitch it is zero.

## The offshore company nobody prices

This is the part the structure pitch leaves out, and it is the only cost here that arrives regardless of scale.

A non-resident person is taxable in the UAE if, among other things, it "Has a nexus in the State as specified in a decision issued by the Cabinet at the suggestion of the Minister". Cabinet Decision No. 35 of 2025 specifies it. A juridical person established under the legislation of a foreign jurisdiction has a nexus where "It derives income from any Immovable Property in the State, and this shall include income derived from the right in rem, sale, disposal, assignment of rights therein, direct use, letting, including subletting and any other form of exploitation of Immovable Property".

Letting is named. Subletting is named. A British Virgin Islands company that owns one Dubai flat and lets it has a nexus in the State from the first dirham of rent, and Article 4 says such a person "shall be required to register for Corporate Tax with the Authority in accordance with Article (51) of the Corporate Tax Law".

The registration is the cost the structure pitch leaves out: Cabinet Decision No. 35 of 2025 gives a foreign company a nexus in the State as soon as it derives income from UAE immovable property, and Article 4 then requires it to register, which is a filing obligation that arrives whether or not any tax is due.

Note also which decision this is. It replaced an earlier one: Cabinet Decision No. 56 of 2023 "shall be repealed but it shall continue to apply to Tax Periods that commenced before 1 January 2025". Any note on offshore ownership written before 2025 is describing a repealed instrument, and the 2025 rule is wider, because it lists two further nexus limbs for investment fund income that the 2023 one did not.

So the offshore holding company does not take a Dubai flat out of the UAE tax net. It puts the flat inside the net, adds a registration, and in all probability produces no tax, because the AED 375,000 band applies to it too.

## What a company can genuinely be for

Not tax, in this jurisdiction. The real reasons are these, and they are real.

**Multiple owners.** Shared ownership between partners or family members is far cleaner as shares in an entity than as names on a deed.

**Succession.** Shares can be dealt with under a will more flexibly than jointly held property, which interacts with [wills and succession](/playbooks/uae-wills-and-succession/).

**Liability separation**, for genuine development or trading activity rather than passive letting.

**Scale.** At a certain portfolio size the administration, financing and governance are simply easier inside an entity, and that size arrives well before the tax does.

Against those, count the costs: formation, annual licensing, audit and filing, potentially different mortgage terms and rates, transfer costs on moving property into the structure, and the tax that was not payable before. Moving a property in triggers the 4% registration fee priced on the [cost of buying](/playbooks/cost-of-buying-in-dubai/) page, which is AED 60,000 on this unit, roughly ten months of its net operating income, before anything else.

## The trap that connects to short letting

Licensed activity is the one route by which an individual loses the carve out. Running holiday homes requires a permit. Brokerage requires a licence. Systematic buying and selling as a business requires one.

Which means the decision in [short let versus long let](/playbooks/short-let-vs-long-let/) is not only an operational one. Converting long lets into licensed holiday homes changes the character of the income, and once the activity requires a licence the real estate investment carve out stops applying and the AED 1,000,000 turnover test is live again. That is a consequence almost nobody models when comparing nightly rates against annual rents.

## The rule of thumb

For one or two properties let on ordinary tenancies, personal ownership is almost always right in the UAE, and the reason is not that a company costs 9%. It is that a company costs formation, licensing, audit and filing to save a tax bill of nothing.

For a genuine business, multiple owners, or a portfolio past the point where governance matters, a structure may earn its keep, and the reasons will be operational. Even there the tax is a rounding error next to the yield: 0.39 points at the ceiling.

One cost of getting into a structure is knowable before any of that. Moving a property you already own into your own company is a registration, and the Land Department charges it at the gift rate of 0.125% of the valuation rather than the 4% it charges on a sale, which is priced line by line on [gifting a Dubai property to family](/playbooks/gifting-a-dubai-property-to-family/). It is the cheapest number in this decision and it is the one the structuring pitch leads with.

Anyone proposing a structure primarily for tax reasons should be asked which tax, on what income, above what threshold. The three figures are AED 375,000, 9%, and on a single flat, zero. Run the income side on the [net rental yield calculator](/calculators/net-rental-yield/) and the tax side is one subtraction away.

## The necessary caveat

This is general information and the rules are recent, detailed and subject to interpretation and change. The 0% band is applied above to a non-resident company with a property nexus on the face of the rate articles, which charge the band on a taxable person's taxable income, and that reading is the first thing to put to an adviser rather than a ruling obtained on these facts. Thresholds, licensing tests and free zone treatment all have specifics this page does not cover. Decisions here belong with a UAE tax adviser, and the cost of that advice is trivial against the cost of structuring wrongly.
