The comparison is made constantly, almost always by a firm that sells one of the two routes, and it starts from a false premise. The two most confident pages ranking on it are titled "why Dubai wins" and "why investors choose Portugal", which tells you where the arithmetic came in.

A golden visa and a D7 are not two prices for the same thing. One is a test on an asset. The other is a test on an income. So "which is cheaper" has no answer, and the question that does have one is this: **how much capital does each test actually need?** That is computable, because the Portuguese threshold is set by instrument and the income a Dubai apartment produces is already modelled on this site. The answer is further down this page and it is not the one either sales pitch gives.

## The asset test, and the three conditions that decide whether you pass it

The Land Department's own golden visa service puts the threshold plainly: it is open to an investor owning property whose purchase value is "equal to or more than 2 million AED at the time of purchase", for a ten year renewable permit.

Three conditions sit under that number, and each one is published.

**It must be yours.** The Ministry of Economy and Tourism's statement of the conditions says the property "must be wholly owned by the investor". Where ownership is a share, the residency authority adds that "the value of the share must not be less than AED 2 million", so a half share of a four million dirham unit qualifies and a half share of a three million dirham unit does not, which is not what splitting a threshold usually means.

**It may not be sold, and the register is made to enforce that.** The residency authority's own page states the condition in terms: **"Property may not be disposed of nor the financial deposit refunded throughout the 10-year residency period."** This is the single most consequential line in the whole comparison, and most pages on the subject do not carry it, or carry something weaker: that the property must still be owned *at renewal*, which would leave the middle of the decade free. The published condition covers the period, not the renewal date.

The same page then says how it is enforced, and this is the sentence worth reading twice: **"It is required to place a lien on the property to ensure the continuity of ownership throughout the validity of the Golden Residency."** So the restriction is not a promise in a policy document that a determined seller could test. It is an encumbrance placed on the property, which means the constraint lives on the register alongside the title rather than in a file at the residency authority. A buyer who has been told the visa is simply a benefit attached to a purchase they already intended to make is being told something incomplete: the purchase changes, because the asset acquires a charge the moment the residency is granted.

**And the mortgage position depends on which government page you read.** The residency authority says "Mortgaged property is acceptable, and includes all types of properties." The Land Department's golden visa service, which is the route a Dubai property owner actually uses, qualifies a mortgaged property only when the application carries "a bank letter indicating 2 million AED paid amount".

Those are not the same rule. On the first, a buyer with a 75% mortgage on a two million dirham unit qualifies on AED 500,000 of equity. On the second, that buyer does not qualify at all, because the paid amount is AED 500,000 and the letter has to say two million. A leveraged buyer reading only the residency authority's page can arrive at the trustee office with the wrong expectation, and this is the kind of discrepancy where the answer is to ask the Land Department in writing before committing to a purchase, not to pick the more convenient page.

## What the asset test costs, as opposed to what it requires

The AED 2,000,000 is a threshold, not a price. The cash that has to be found is the price, plus the entry stack the [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/) page itemises to the instrument that sets each line, plus the visa's own fees, which the Land Department publishes itemised.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Medical examination | 700 |
| Emirates ID, ten years | 1,153 |
| Confirmation of residency permit | 2,856.75 |
| Land Department fees | 4,020 |
| Administrative fees | 1,155 |
| **Total, applicant** | **9,884.75** |

Each dependant is a further AED 5,774.50, with a file opening fee of AED 318.75. Those figures are the Land Department's, and the better pages on golden visa costs already reproduce them correctly, so this table is not this page's contribution to the subject.

Putting it together on an apartment at exactly the threshold, which is the same building as the illustrative unit used throughout this site at the same price per square foot and the same rent per square foot, so 1,200 sq ft at AED 2,000,000 letting for AED 140,000:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Price | 2,000,000 |
| Entry stack, from the fee schedule | 126,720 |
| Golden visa fees, applicant | 9,884.75 |
| **Cash required** | **2,136,604.75** |
| **Of which never comes back** | **136,604.75** |
| Per year of residency, over ten years | 13,660.48 |

So the visa's own fees run to under a thousand dirhams a year and the entry stack is thirteen times larger. The residency is cheap; the transaction that qualifies you for it is not. The apartment nets 4.36% on that outlay, which is the ordinary answer the [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/) page gives, and the residency does not improve it.

What the table cannot price is the disposal condition, and nothing can, because its cost is the sale you would otherwise have made and did not. The honest statement of it is the one the [price index](/dubai-price-index/) makes available: across the 159 months of Land Department data, the index fell 18.1% over one six year stretch and rose 54.8% over a three year one. A ten year commitment made at an arbitrary point in that series is a bet that the decade ends somewhere you would have been willing to sell. [Liquidity risk](/playbooks/liquidity-risk/) is the framework for what that costs, and [emergency liquidity](/playbooks/emergency-liquidity/) is the reserve that has to sit behind it.

## The income test, and why its threshold is not a number anybody published

Portugal's property route is gone. [Lei n.º 56/2023](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/56-2023-222477692) of 6 October 2023 states its own purpose, in the list in Artigo 1.º, as proceeding to the "revogacao das autorizacoes de residencia para atividade de investimento imobiliario", which is the revocation of residence permits for real estate investment activity. Artigo 44.º carries the amendment to the immigration law, Artigo 43.º deals with applications already pending, and Artigo 55.º sets when it came into force. Buying a house in Portugal today buys a house.

Which is why the D7 gets recommended in its place, usually by people who have not noticed it is a different instrument. It does not ask what you own. It asks what arrives each month, and whether you intend to be there.

Its threshold is the thing nearly every guide gets slightly wrong, because it is not a visa figure at all. [Portaria n.º 1563/2007](https://vistos.mne.gov.pt/images/schengen/portaria1563_2007_meios_de_subsist.pdf) sets the means of subsistence as a proportion of the national minimum wage: 100% for the first adult, "Segundo ou mais adultos 50" per cent, and 30% for children under eighteen and dependent adult children. The minimum wage is then set annually, and for 2026 it is EUR 920 a month, under **Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2025** of 29 December 2025.

So the threshold is arithmetic, and it moves every January:

| Household | A month | A year | A year, in dirhams |
|---|---|---|---|
| One applicant | 920.00 | 11,040.00 | 45,511 |
| Applicant and spouse | 1,380.00 | 16,560.00 | 68,267 |
| Applicant, spouse and one child | 1,656.00 | 19,872.00 | 81,920 |

Converted at the European Central Bank's euro reference rate of 1.1225 dollars on 2 October 2026 and the dirham's peg of 3.6725 to the dollar, which is AED 4.1224 to the euro. A guide quoting last year's euro figure is quoting the wrong threshold, and a guide quoting a dirham figure without a dated rate is quoting two wrong numbers.

## The sum nobody does

Here is the comparison, in the only unit that makes the two tests commensurable.

The asset test needs AED 2,000,000 of property. The income test needs a property whose net income reaches the threshold, so solve for it. Take the same apartment, scale it by area at the same price and rent per square foot, and find the smallest one whose net operating income, computed by the same module the rest of this site is checked against, clears a couple's EUR 16,560 a year.

It is **890 sq ft, at AED 1,483,333**, netting AED 68,342 against a threshold of AED 68,267, a surplus of AED 75, which is what a solve rather than a rounded figure looks like.

| Route | Capital required |
|---|---|
| Asset test, Dubai golden visa | 2,000,000 |
| Income test, Portugal D7, couple | 1,483,333 |
| **Difference** | **516,667** |

The income test clears on 74.2% of the capital the asset test demands. And it is not the same kind of capital: the AED 1,483,333 can be sold on any day of the ten years, and the AED 2,000,000 cannot.

That is the finding, and it reverses the framing both sales pitches use. The route sold as the investor's route asks for more money and takes away the right to move it. The route sold as the retiree's modest alternative asks for less and leaves the asset liquid.

There is a further way to read the same arithmetic, and it is the one most likely to be useful. The illustrative one bedroom this site has modelled from the beginning, 900 sq ft at AED 1,500,000 letting for AED 105,000, nets AED 69,127, which is EUR 16,769 a year, or EUR 1,397 a month.

| Household | Threshold, a year | What this one apartment covers |
|---|---|---|
| One applicant | 45,511 | 151.9% |
| Applicant and spouse | 68,267 | 101.3% |
| Applicant, spouse and one child | 81,920 | 84.4% |

One ordinary Dubai one bedroom is, to within 1.3%, exactly a Portugal D7 for a couple. A child tips it short by AED 12,793 a year, which on the same terms is a 164 sq ft larger apartment at AED 1,773,333, solved the same way, and still AED 226,667 below the golden visa threshold. Nobody asking whether their Dubai rent can get them into Europe has been given that number, and it turns out to be the one they were asking for.

The caveat that keeps the arithmetic honest: the threshold is a floor, not a target. Applications are assessed on the stability and provenance of the income and on savings held alongside it, so clearing 101.3% on paper is not the same position as clearing 150%, and a single tenancy that ends is a threshold that fails. The published minimum is where the assessment starts, not where it finishes.

## The asymmetry that runs the other way

Everything above favours the income test. One thing does not, and it is large.

A D7 holder lives in Portugal and becomes resident there for tax. Portugal's tax authority states the consequence directly: under **Artigo 15.º** of the personal income tax code, a resident declares all income, obtained in Portugal and abroad alike. Relief for tax already paid at source comes through the credit in **Artigo 81.º**.

A credit is worth what the source country charged. The UAE levies no personal income tax on residential rent, as [residency and tax](/playbooks/residency-and-tax/) sets out, so the credit on Dubai rental income is nil and the rent arrives in the Portuguese computation gross. The income that qualified you for the visa is taxed by the country that granted it, at whatever rate and under whichever category and election applies to a foreign let, which is a question for a Portuguese adviser and not a number this page will invent.

The Dubai golden visa does none of that. It taxes neither the rent nor the gain.

So the two routes are not merely different tests. They are different tests with opposite after-tax consequences, and the order to decide in is: establish whether the goal is an asset, a place to live, or an eventual passport, because the three lead to different answers. Portugal's residence leads to permanent residence at five years and citizenship at ten with a language requirement. The golden visa is long term residence and is not a naturalisation path in the way people often assume. And if the goal is an investment return, neither should be driving the purchase, because a property bought for a visa is a property bought for a reason that has nothing to do with its [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/), and if the unit is a bad purchase a residency does not repair it, it locks you into it for ten years. Run it through the [calculator](/calculators/net-rental-yield/) on its own merits first.

## Why this page carries a heavier warning than most

Because the ground moves, and it has moved twice inside three years.

The UAE's own official sources still do not read identically on duration. The residency authority and the Land Department both set out ten years for the two million dirham property route. The federal government portal lists five years against real estate investment, alongside the ten year route. Those may be describing different categories, and a reader who finds it confusing is reading it correctly.

That is the point worth taking away. When two government sources describe a rule differently, a blog post confidently reconciling them is not a source. This is the framework in the library that most needs a licensed adviser attached to it, in both jurisdictions, on the day you act. [Residency and tax](/playbooks/residency-and-tax/) covers what a move does and does not change, and [the year you sell](/playbooks/the-year-you-sell/) covers the timing that is settled before you arrive and cannot be reopened afterwards.
