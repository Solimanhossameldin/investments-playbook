Search for freehold areas in Dubai and every result is a list of community names. The lists run to thirty or forty entries. They name places the designating instrument does not mention. None of them carries a source, and none of them carries a date, which for a list that can only change by a decision of the Ruler is the defect that matters most.

The instrument is [Regulation No. (3) of 2006](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2006/Regulation%20No.%20(3)%20of%202006.html), issued on 7 June 2006. It does not designate communities. It designates numbered land plots.

## What the regulation actually says

Article 3 is one sentence, and it grants three different things at once:

> Non-UAE Nationals may acquire **freehold ownership rights over Real Property without time restriction**, usufruct rights, or **leasehold rights for up to ninety-nine (99) years**, **with respect to the land plot(s) indicated opposite each of the following areas** **as shown by the maps issued by the Department** and attached to this Regulation.

Three things follow from that sentence, and all three are missed by the lists.

**The grant is plot-level.** The regulation names areas only as headings for plot numbers. Dubai Marina appears with plots 007, 014, 015 and 033 beside it, not as a community. Jebel Ali appears with nine plots. An area name in this regulation is an index entry, and the designation attaches to the numbered plot.

**The maps govern, not the text.** The plots are designated "as shown by the maps issued by the Department" and attached to the regulation. Where a plot boundary is the question, the map is the instrument and the list of names is not evidence of anything.

**Freehold and leasehold are granted in the same breath.** The article permits a non-UAE national to take freehold without time restriction, or a usufruct, or a lease of up to ninety-nine years, over the same designated plot. Which one is being sold is a term of the transaction, not a property of the postcode. The second half of this page is about what that difference is worth, because it is the part nobody prices.

[Law No. (7) of 2006](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2006/Law%20No.%20(7)%20of%202006.html) is the parent. Article 4 restricts ownership to UAE and GCC nationals and their wholly owned companies, and permits non-UAE nationals to be granted freehold or a term "in certain areas determined by the Ruler". So the designation is a sovereign act. That is why the regulation has no amendment article: it is not amended, it is added to, by a further decision of the same kind.

Twenty-three areas and forty-five plots. Then Article 4, which is the regulation's own demonstration that the three interests are not interchangeable:

> Non-UAE nationals may acquire usufruct rights or leasehold rights for up to ninety-nine (99) years **in plot no. (224) of the Nad al-Sheba area** as highlighted by the Department in the map attached to this Regulation.

One plot, next door to three plots that carry the full freehold grant, and on it a foreign buyer may take a term and nothing more. A list of area names cannot express that distinction, which is the clearest evidence that a list of area names is the wrong instrument to be reading.

Article 5 says the regulation "will come into force on the day on which it is published" in the Official Gazette. There is no transitional provision and no sunset.

## Why the list is not the answer, and what is

A published list of freehold areas has three problems at once. It is at the wrong level of detail, because designation is by plot. It has no mechanism for staying current, because additions are made by separate sovereign decision rather than by amending this regulation. And it is undated, so a reader cannot tell which vintage they are looking at.

The register has none of those problems. Article 7 of Law No. (7) of 2006 gives the Property Register "absolute evidentiary value against all parties", and Article 9 makes an unrecorded transaction invalid rather than merely unregistered. So the question "can a foreigner own this" has a single authoritative answer for one specific unit, it is free, and it takes minutes: the Land Department's [Verify Title Deed](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/title-deed-verification-overview/) service shows what the register says, including the nature of the interest.

That check is the first of the four binary checks set out in [due diligence before an offer](/playbooks/due-diligence-before-an-offer/). It costs nothing and, failed, costs the entire outlay: on the illustrative apartment that is the price plus an acquisition stack of AED 96,220, itemised to the instrument that sets each line on [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/). Set that against the AED 30,000 registration-fee split which is the one thing every buyer in Dubai negotiates, and the ranking is the same one this site keeps arriving at from different directions. The free checks protect multiples of what the argued-over fee is worth.

## What a term is worth, which is the part nobody computes

Now the arithmetic. If Article 3 lets the same plot be sold freehold or on a 99-year lease, a buyer should know what the lease is worth relative to the freehold, and that is a solved problem rather than a matter of opinion.

A perpetual net income of R capitalised at rate r is worth R divided by r. The same income for n years is worth an n-year annuity, R times (1 minus (1 plus r) to the power of minus n) divided by r. Divide one by the other and the rate cancels out of the numerator:

**share of freehold value = 1 minus (1 + r) to the power of minus n**

The capitalisation rate used below is the illustrative apartment's own net income over its price alone, **4.61%**, which is the yield a valuer would capitalise and the middle of the three yields computed on the [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/) page. The unit is the one used throughout this site: AED 1,500,000, 900 sq ft, AED 105,000 rent.

| Years left on the term | Share of freehold value | Worth on this unit | Discount to freehold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 99 | 98.85% | 1,482,688 | 17,312 |
| 90 | 98.27% | 1,474,028 | 25,972 |
| 79 | 97.16% | 1,457,361 | 42,639 |
| 50 | 89.50% | 1,342,445 | 157,555 |
| 30 | 74.13% | 1,111,943 | 388,057 |
| 20 | 59.40% | 890,986 | 609,014 |
| 10 | 36.28% | 544,217 | 955,783 |

## The finding

At the moment of grant a 99-year lease is worth **98.85%** of the freehold. That is why the distinction looks academic, and why it is treated as a labelling difference in practice.

But the term runs. A 99-year lease granted when this regulation came into force in 2006 has **79 years** left in 2026, and is worth **97.16%** of freehold, a discount of **AED 42,639** on this apartment.

**That discount is already larger than the registration-fee split every buyer in Dubai argues about**, and it grows every year without anyone doing anything. By the time 30 years remain the arithmetic says the interest is worth 74.13% of the freehold, a gap of AED 388,057, which is more than twelve times the fee argument and larger than the entire acquisition stack.

Two honest qualifications, because this is the kind of claim that deserves them. The first is that this is a valuation argument, not an observation: it says what the arithmetic implies, not what Dubai buyers currently pay. If term-limited interests trade here at no discount, then either the market is mispricing duration or there is a local feature the ratio does not capture, such as a renewal expectation, and a buyer's job is to establish which rather than to assume the comfortable one. The second is that the rate does the work. At a 7% capitalisation rate a 30-year term is worth 86.9% of freehold rather than 74.13%, so the discount is only ever as firm as the yield it is taken at.

What is not in doubt is the direction. A term is worth less than a perpetuity, by an amount that is computable from figures a buyer already has, and the computation takes one line.

## What to do with this

Ask for the plot number, not the community name. Verify the title deed before the second viewing, which settles both whether the plot is designated and which of the three interests is on offer. If the answer is a term rather than freehold, establish the years remaining and run the ratio before agreeing a price, then price the purchase with the [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/) arithmetic on the discounted value rather than the asking price. If the asset is a term and the term is short, [the minimum hold period](/glossary/minimum-hold-period/) matters more than usual, because a shortening lease is a depreciating asset and the round trip has to be absorbed inside a window that is closing.

And treat any list of freehold areas, including the table above, as what it is: the regulation as issued, useful for understanding how the designation works and insufficient for deciding whether to buy one specific flat.
