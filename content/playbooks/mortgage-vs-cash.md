On the illustrative one bedroom this site uses throughout, **AED 1,500,000**, 900 square feet, let at AED 105,000 a year, with **AED 69,127** of net operating income, borrowing at 75% loan to value raises the return on your own money at any mortgage rate **below 4.30%**, and lowers it above. That is the whole decision, and the threshold is lower than every yield a buyer is shown.

Most buyers frame this as whether they can afford to pay cash, which is the wrong question: paying cash is always affordable if you have the cash. The right question is what each dirham of borrowing earns, and the answer is a rate you can compare against the one a bank quotes you.

## The rule everyone prints, and the yield it uses

Every competing page on this query states the same rule in some form: borrow when the rental yield beats the mortgage rate. The shape is right. Every published version of the yield is wrong, because the two sides of the comparison are not measured on the same money.

| What the rule gets compared against | Value | Overstates the threshold by, points |
|---|---|---|
| Gross yield | 7.00% | 2.70 |
| Net income over the price alone | 4.61% | 0.31 |
| Net yield, over price plus the acquisition stack | 4.33% | 0.03 |
| **The break-even mortgage rate** | **4.30%** |, |

The first row is the one that actually does the damage, because the gross yield is the number a listing carries and the number the portals quote by community. A buyer comparing a 4.5% mortgage against a 7% gross yield concludes there is two and a half points of room. There is none: at 4.5% the loan is already lowering the return on their own money.

## Why the threshold sits below all three

The cash buyer's money is the price plus the whole acquisition stack, **AED 96,220** on this flat, itemised line by instrument on [net rental yield](/playbooks/net-rental-yield/). The borrower's money is the deposit, plus that same stack, plus the **AED 7,283** that registering the mortgage costs in its own fee, trustee and title deed.

That last block is the part the rule drops. It cannot be borrowed, and the reason is structural rather than a lender's preference: the loan to value cap is a percentage of the value of the property, and a fee is not the property. So the mortgage's own fees sit in the borrower's denominator and in nobody's loan, which is the same mechanism that makes an advertised deposit smaller than the cash actually needed on the day, worked through on [how much you can borrow](/playbooks/mortgage-capacity/).

Set the two returns equal and solve for the rate. The algebra collapses to one line:

**break-even rate = net yield on total cash outlay × (1 − mortgage fees ÷ loan)**

On this flat that is 4.33% × (1 − 7,283 ÷ 1,125,000), or **4.30%**. Solving the two-sided equation numerically gives the same figure, which is the check that the derivation and the table below are the same piece of arithmetic.

## The worked example, rate by rate

Interest only, deliberately. A repayment instalment also returns capital, which is saving rather than cost, and netting the whole instalment against income would overstate the drag. The buyer puts in **AED 478,503** of their own money, AED 375,000 of deposit and AED 103,503 of fees, against **AED 1,596,220** for the cash buyer.

| Mortgage rate | Annual interest | Income after interest | Return on your own money | Against 4.33% cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.00% | 33,750 | 35,377 | 7.39% | Borrowing wins |
| 3.50% | 39,375 | 29,752 | 6.22% | Borrowing wins |
| 4.00% | 45,000 | 24,127 | 5.04% | Borrowing wins |
| **4.30%** | **48,405** | **20,722** | **4.33%** | **Level, the break-even rate** |
| 4.50% | 50,625 | 18,502 | 3.87% | Cash wins |
| 5.00% | 56,250 | 12,877 | 2.69% | Cash wins |
| 5.50% | 61,875 | 7,252 | 1.52% | Cash wins |
| 6.00% | 67,500 | 1,627 | 0.34% | Cash wins |

Two separate things stop being true at two different rates, and they are routinely confused. Above **4.30%** the loan lowers the return on your own money. The rent still covers the interest, so nothing bounces; the money is simply working less hard than it would have unlevered. Only above **6.14%** does the rent stop covering the interest at all, and from there the shortfall is funded out of salary whatever the asset does.

## The threshold hardly moves with the size of the loan

This is the part that contradicts how leverage is usually described. The first factor in the formula contains no loan at all, and the second contains it only through the fixed part of the mortgage fee schedule spread over a larger balance.

| Loan to value | Loan | Your own money in | Break-even rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | 750,000 | 852,565 | 4.29% |
| 60% | 900,000 | 702,940 | 4.30% |
| 70% | 1,050,000 | 553,315 | 4.30% |
| 75% | 1,125,000 | 478,503 | 4.30% |
| 80% | 1,200,000 | 403,690 | 4.30% |

**One basis point across a sixty per cent swing in borrowing.** Borrowing more does not buy a more forgiving break-even rate. What it buys is a larger multiple of the same answer: at a rate below the threshold, more leverage lifts the return further, and at a rate above it, more leverage destroys it faster. The decision about the rate and the decision about the size are separate, and only the second one is about risk.

## What the regulation allows

The caps are not a lender's policy. They are Article 3 of the Central Bank's [Regulations Regarding Mortgage Loans](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/article-3-important-ratios), Circular No. 31/2013, in the consolidated text of 8 April 2020 after the amendments made by Board Resolutions 96/2019 and 31/2/2020.

| Borrower and property | Maximum loan to value |
|---|---|
| Expatriate, first home, below AED 5 million | 80% |
| Expatriate, first home, above AED 5 million | 70% |
| Expatriate, second or subsequent | 60% |
| Anything bought off plan | 50% |

In the article's own words the first band is **maximum 80% of the value of the property**, the second-property band is **60% of the value of the property, regardless of value**, and off plan is absolute: **the maximum LTV for mortgages on property being purchased off plans is 50% regardless of purpose, value, or category of purchaser**.

Those figures were amended upward. The original 2013 text put the expatriate bands at 75%, 65% and 60%, and summaries of the regulation written before April 2020, along with a good many written since, still carry the old numbers. The 75% used throughout this page is an illustration sitting inside the 80% band, not the band itself.

The cap is also rarely the thing that binds. Article 3 sets the debt burden ratio at **50 percent of gross salary and any regular income from a defined and specific source at any time**, caps the tenor at **The maximum tenor of the mortgage loan is 25 years**, and limits an expatriate to **Expatriates: up to 7 years annual income**. Which of the three decides your loan is itself a calculation, and [mortgage capacity](/playbooks/mortgage-capacity/) solves for the stressed rate at which the binding cap changes hands.

## What leverage does that no yield shows

It multiplies price movements against your equity rather than against the price. At 75% loan to value a 15% fall in value removes 60% of the deposit before any selling cost, and the same arithmetic run across the loan to value bands is what makes a down-valuation cost the best-financed buyer the most cash, set out on [when the valuation comes in low](/playbooks/when-the-valuation-comes-in-low/) and sized against a portfolio on [position sizing](/playbooks/position-sizing/).

It also converts a flexible asset into a fixed obligation. A cash buyer facing a long vacancy has a disappointing year. A leveraged buyer facing the same vacancy has a payment due on the first of the month regardless, which is why [break-even occupancy](/playbooks/break-even-occupancy/) is the companion calculation rather than an optional one.

## How to actually decide

Three numbers, in this order.

Recompute the threshold on your own flat, not on this one. It is net operating income over price plus the acquisition stack, times one minus the mortgage fees over the loan, and the input it is most sensitive to is the service charge, [the figures approved for 31 named buildings](/service-charges/) show the spread, and [the calculator](/calculators/net-rental-yield/) will run it.

Then get the all-in rate, including arrangement, valuation and insurance, not the headline rate. Then subtract. If the all-in rate is above the threshold, the loan lowers the return on your own money, and the case for it rests entirely on capital growth, which is a defensible position, but it should be said out loud rather than smuggled in through a gross yield.

Then stress it. Re-run at three points higher, at twelve months empty, and at both. If any of those cannot be funded from income you already have, the loan is larger than the position, whatever the threshold says. And when the fixed period ends, the whole calculation is done again at the new rate, which is the arithmetic [refinancing a Dubai mortgage](/playbooks/refinancing-a-dubai-mortgage/) carries through to the exit costs.
